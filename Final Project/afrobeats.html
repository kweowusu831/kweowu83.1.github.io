Afrobeats: The Sound Taking Over the World

We’ve explored Hip-Hop and R&B, but what if I told you there’s another genre one that many in the Western world have yet to fully discover that’s quietly taking over the globe? you might look at me like I’m crazy, but trust me: Afrobeats is here, and it’s changing music as we know it.

So, what exactly is Afrobeats? This energetic and soulful genre originated in West Africa during the 1960s and 70s. It emerged as a fusion of traditional Yoruba music, smooth jazz, funk, and a style of Ghanaian music called Highlife. What began as a cultural sound is now a global movement, bringing African rhythm, storytelling, and culture to the world stage.

Tracing the Roots of Afrobeat

To understand Afrobeat, you have to dig deeper than the beats and melodies, you have to understand the movement. Much of the genre’s foundation is credited to the legendary Nigerian artist and activist Fela Kuti.

Fela wasn’t just a musician, he was a revolutionary. Influenced by jazz, soul, and traditional African music, he developed a bold new sound that would become known as Afrobeats. But his music was more than entertainment. After a transformative trip to the United States in the 1960s, where he met civil rights activist Sandra Smith, Fela was exposed to the works of Martin Luther King Jr. and Malcolm X. This awakened a political consciousness in him, and he used his music as a platform to fight corruption and call out social injustice in Nigeria.

One of the most important collaborators in Fela’s success was drummer Tony Allen, often considered one of the greatest drummers of all time. His unmatched rhythm brought a hypnotic pulse to the music, blending Ghanaian Highlife with traditional Nigerian beats. Allen, of both Nigerian and Ghanaian heritage, helped create a style that was unlike anything the world had heard at the time.

In 2009, Fela’s legacy was brought to Broadway in the musical FELA!, produced by icons Jay-Z and Will Smith. The production helped introduce a new generation to the sound, history, and impact of Afrobeats laying the foundation for its global rise today.

Highlife: The Hidden Root of Afrobeat

It’s important to note that the genre’s roots go even further back. In the 1920s, a Ghanaian style called Highlife laid the foundation for what would become Afrobeats. While Fela Kuti is widely recognized as the father of the genre, the musical influences of both Nigeria and Ghana played crucial roles in shaping the sound.

Afrobeat vs. Afrobeats: Yes, There's a Difference

It’s important to note that there’s a big difference between Afrobeat and Afrobeats. Yes, it sounds silly at first. You might think the only distinction is a missing “s.” But that’s far from the truth.

Afrobeat (singular) refers to the genre pioneered by Fela Kuti a fusion of jazz, funk, traditional African rhythms, and politically charged lyrics. It was deeply tied to activism and served as a soundtrack for revolution, challenging injustices in Nigeria and beyond.

Afrobeats (plural), on the other hand, is the evolution of that legacy. It’s what we hear today blending highlife, dancehall, hip-hop, R&B, and pop with smooth, electric beats. The political undertones are still there for some artists, but Afrobeats today is more global, danceable, and commercially versatile.

Afrobeats Generations

Foundational Artists

Don Jazzy

Don Jazzy is one of the most sensational producers in the Afrobeats game. He is not just a hitmaker but a master of crafting phenomenal beats and producing some of the genre's most iconic songs. As the godfather of modern Afrobeats and founder of Mavin Records, Don Jazzy has helped launch the careers of superstars like Rema and Ayra Starr. His voice is so distinct that fans instantly recognize it. Since the 2000s, he has contributed to many major hits and worked with numerous artists featured on this list.

P-Square

P-Square is one of the most iconic duos in Afrobeats. Comprised of twin brothers Peter and Paul Okoye, they brought global attention to the genre with their catchy melodies, electrifying performances, and songs about love and celebration. Despite being veterans in the game, they remain relevant and beloved across Africa. They've blended R&B, pop, and Afro rhythms to help define an entire era of music.

Flavour

Flavour is a pioneer whose voice is instantly recognizable and known for melting hearts. He blends the nostalgic highlife sound of the 1920s with modern flair, creating unforgettable tracks that bridge generations. His music mixes traditional Igbo rhythms with romance and gospel elements, and he often uses his platform to praise and uplift.

Icons in a League of Their Own

Davido

The son of a billionaire, Davido broke expectations by pursuing music instead of following a corporate path. His drive and passion made him a global Afrobeats ambassador. With his infectious charisma and countless hits, he's played a crucial role in bringing Afrobeats to stadiums and arenas around the world.

Wizkid

Wizkid is the golden boy of Afrobeats. His smooth, effortless style and global collaborations with artists like Drake and Beyoncé have helped take the genre to new heights. Despite his international success, he remains grounded in his Nigerian roots, which makes his sound authentic and unforgettable.

The New Wave

Burna Boy

Burna Boy, also known as the African Giant, is a dynamic force in modern Afrobeats. He effortlessly blends Afro-fusion with reggae, dancehall, and hip-hop. His lyrics are powerful, and his Grammy win was not just for him, but for the entire continent. Burna's versatility is a testament to the genre's global evolution.

Rema

Rema is the future of Afrobeats. Young and versatile, he has experimented with hip-hop, pop, and even rock, all while proudly representing Benin City, Nigeria. His global anthem "Calm Down" has been played at world events, making him one of the most recognizable new stars in music.

Asake

Asake stormed the scene with his chant-like vocals and explosive energy. He blends the traditional Fuji sound with South Africa's amapiano style, creating high-octane tracks that dominate dance floors. His music ignites adrenaline and celebrates African culture with every beat.

Tems

Tems brings a soulful, ethereal sound that has redefined the boundaries of Afrobeats. Blending R&B with Afro rhythms, her Grammy-winning music captures raw emotion and introspection. Her unique voice and songwriting have introduced Afrobeats to new audiences while keeping its cultural essence alive.

What Does All of This Mean?

Afrobeats is more than a genre- it’s a cultural force. Born from the revolutionary spirit of Afrobeat, shaped by the rhythms of Highlife, and driven by a new generation of boundary-pushing artists, Afrobeats has become a global movement. It celebrates African excellence, innovation, and joy while continuing to inspire the world.

Whether it's the legacy of Fela Kuti or the global rise of Burna Boy, Afrobeats proves that music is one of the most powerful ways to tell a story and Africa has a lot to say.
