The Global Impact of Black Music

Now, despite the fact that this website was created to showcase some genres of music that I personally love, there has always been a message behind it: music is universal.

As we've seen, Afrobeat was born out of jazz and activism. Fela Kuti's time in the U.S. exposed him to soul and political thought that helped shape his sound. R&B flowed into hip-hop, and Afrobeats now connects artists across continents. The collaborations we see today from Rema and Selena Gomez to Wizkid and Brent Faiyaz are proof that music has no borders. Although I am of African descent, my love for music crosses genres and geographies. I love jazz, R&B, hip-hop, Afrobeats because music isn’t about where you’re from, it’s about how you connect and what you can teach others. Music is not confined to one lane, it’s a global language that speaks to all.
